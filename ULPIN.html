Introduction

What is ULPIN? The Aadhaar for Your Land

ULPIN — the Unique Land Parcel Identification Number, also known as Bhu-Aadhar — is a 14-digit alphanumeric code permanently assigned to every land parcel in India. Just as Aadhaar gives every citizen a unique identity, ULPIN gives every piece of land its own permanent digital identity.

It is generated from the precise latitude and longitude geo-coordinates of the parcel, making it location-specific, tamper-proof, and globally unique. No two parcels can ever share the same ULPIN.

XXXX-XXXX-XXXX-XX 14-digit geo-spatial code · Permanent · Based on GPS coordinates · Internationally compliant (ECCMA / OGC)

ULPIN is implemented under the Digital India Land Records Modernisation Programme (DILRMP) by the Department of Land Resources, Government of India. It aims to create a single, authoritative, nationally unified record of land ownership across all states.

In simple terms: Your plot's ULPIN is its permanent address in the government's digital system — linked to your name, boundaries, area, and ownership history — accessible by every department that needs it.

Importance

Why ULPIN Matters — 6 Reasons It Protects Landowners

  • 🛡️

    Stops fake surveys and encroachment

    A geo-referenced official record means any private or unauthorised "measurement" has no legal standing. ULPIN establishes the exact boundary of your parcel in government records — overriding any informal claim.

  • 📄

    One ID accepted across all departments

    Banks, courts, revenue offices, and registration departments all reference the same ULPIN. No more inconsistencies between old survey numbers, taluka records, and city survey data.

  • 💰

    Faster loans and property transactions

    Banks can instantly verify ownership and title through ULPIN, reducing loan processing from weeks to hours. Clear digital records eliminate disputes and speed up registration.

  • 🌾

    Access to agricultural schemes

    PM-Kisan, crop insurance, soil health cards, and irrigation subsidies are increasingly linked to verified digital land records. ULPIN ensures farmers are not excluded due to mismatched records.

  • 👨‍👩‍👧

    Clean inheritance and mutation

    When land changes hands — by sale, inheritance, or court order — the ULPIN stays with the parcel. Ownership is updated digitally, removing ambiguity about which survey number is the "correct" one.

  • 🌐

    Internationally recognised standard

    ULPIN adheres to ECCMA and Open Geospatial Consortium (OGC) international standards, making Indian land records compatible with global finance and commerce systems.

How it works

How is a ULPIN Generated?

ULPIN generation is not a self-service process. It follows a systematic government workflow:

Stage 1

Cadastral map digitisation

The village's traditional survey (cadastral) maps are scanned and converted into digital format by the state Land Records department.

Stage 2

Geo-referencing

Each parcel on the digital map is assigned precise GPS latitude/longitude coordinates. This is the mandatory prerequisite — without it, no ULPIN can be generated.

Stage 3

ULPIN code generation

The 14-digit ULPIN is algorithmically generated from the parcel's geo-coordinates and linked to the land record (RoR / 7-12 extract) in the state database.

Stage 4

Integration with land records

The ULPIN appears on the land record and is shared with central databases (DILRMP), making it queryable by banks, courts, and other agencies.

Important: Geo-referencing of the cadastral map is a hard requirement. No geo-referencing = no ULPIN. If your village's maps have not been digitised yet, the process starts there.

Check your status

How to Check if Your Land Has a ULPIN

Visit your state's official land records portal and search by your survey number, gat number, or owner name. Most portals now display ULPIN where it has been assigned.

Mahabhulekh — Maharashtra Bhoomi — Karnataka Bhulekh — Uttar Pradesh Bhulekh — Odisha Dharitree — Assam Jamabandi — Haryana Apna Khata — Rajasthan dilrmp.gov.in — National

Search "[your state] land records portal" to find the correct state website. On Maharashtra's Mahabhulekh (mahabhulekh.maharashtra.gov.in), search using your 7/12 (Satbara) extract details.

Note: As of early 2024, only about 44% of rural parcels have been assigned ULPINs. If yours does not appear yet, it does not mean there is a problem — it may simply mean your village's geo-referencing is still pending.

Action guide

What to Do If Your Land Has Not Been Assigned a ULPIN

You cannot apply for a ULPIN yourself — the government assigns it systematically. But there are clear steps to ensure you are included correctly when the process reaches your area.

Step 1

Check the state portal first

Visit your state land records portal and search your plot. Confirm whether ULPIN has been assigned or is still pending for your village.

Step 2

Visit your tehsil / taluka office

Carry your 7/12 extract (or RoR), sale deed, and identity proof. Ask the patwari or revenue officer about the ULPIN status and geo-referencing progress for your village.

Step 3

Fix any record discrepancies first

Errors in your base land record (wrong name, area, or boundary) carry over into ULPIN. Get mutation or corrections done before ULPIN assignment reaches your area.

Step 4

Request geo-referencing

If your village cadastral map has not been geo-referenced, formally request the District Superintendent of Land Records to prioritise it.

Step 5

Escalate if delayed

If tehsil is unresponsive, escalate to the District Collector's office. You may also file a grievance at pgportal.gov.in (PM Grievance Portal) or your state's grievance portal.

Step 6

Keep certified documents safe

Until your ULPIN is assigned, maintain certified copies of your 7/12, sale deed, and property tax receipts. These remain your legal proof in the interim.

Land protection

ULPIN as Protection Against Fake Surveys and Land Fraud

One of the most significant protections ULPIN offers is making unauthorised and fake surveys legally invalid. In many parts of India, land disputes arise because plot boundaries are contested through informal, private measurements — sometimes by individuals with vested interests.

When a parcel has an official ULPIN with geo-referenced coordinates, its boundaries exist in the government's digital system. Any claim that contradicts those coordinates — regardless of who measured what, when — has no legal basis. The official record wins.

This is why areas not yet included in City Survey or official land record systems remain vulnerable: without an official geo-reference, there is no digital anchor for ownership. ULPIN is that anchor.

Key principle: Any survey conducted without an authorised government Land Records officer is illegal under the Maharashtra Land Revenue Code and equivalent state laws. ULPIN geo-data is the standard against which all boundary claims are measured.

ULPIN (भू-आधार) म्हणजे काय? — थोडक्यात

ULPIN — जमिनीचा आधार क्रमांक

ULPIN (युनिक लँड पार्सल आयडेंटिफिकेशन नंबर) म्हणजेच भू-आधार — भारतातील प्रत्येक जमिनीच्या तुकड्यासाठी दिला जाणारा १४ अंकी विशिष्ट क्रमांक. हा क्रमांक त्या जमिनीच्या अक्षांश-रेखांश (GPS) निर्देशांकांवरून तयार होतो.

जसा आधार क्रमांक प्रत्येक नागरिकाची ओळख ठरवतो, तसाच ULPIN प्रत्येक जमिनीची कायदेशीर ओळख ठरवतो. यामुळे बनावट मोजणी, अतिक्रमण, आणि जमीन हडप या गोष्टींना आळा घालणे शासनाला शक्य होते.

ULPIN तपासायचे कसे? तुमच्या राज्याच्या भूमि अभिलेख संकेतस्थळावर जा — महाराष्ट्रासाठी mahabhulekh.maharashtra.gov.in — आणि सातबारा उताऱ्याद्वारे शोधा. जर ULPIN अद्याप मिळाला नसेल, तर तहसील कार्यालयात जाऊन जमिनीची नोंद दुरुस्त करा व भूमापन विभागाकडे भू-संदर्भन (geo-referencing) करण्याची विनंती करा.
